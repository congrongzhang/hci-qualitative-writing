# Qualitative Evidence Boundaries

Use this guide to calibrate language, protect participant meaning, and keep interpretation auditable.

## Evidence states

Distinguish these states in notes and prose:

1. **Participant report:** what a participant said, recalled, believed, or reported.
2. **Observed or documented event:** what the researcher directly observed or what a source records.
3. **Within-case interpretation:** the researcher's explanation of material from one case.
4. **Cross-case pattern:** a relationship established through comparison across cases or materials.
5. **Theoretical extension:** an abstraction that connects the analysis to a wider scholarly explanation.
6. **Practical implication:** an action-oriented inference derived from findings and explicit values.

Do not silently move from one state to another. Use transitions that identify the analytic step.

## Calibrate verbs

- Participant reports: `described`, `reported`, `recalled`, `perceived`, `interpreted`.
- Observed patterns: `showed`, `documented`, `identified`, `traced`, `revealed`.
- Scoped interpretations: `indicates`, `suggests`, `supports the interpretation`, `is consistent with`.
- Conceptual advances: `distinguishes`, `refines`, `extends`, `qualifies`, `reframes`.

Reserve causal verbs such as `caused`, `led to`, and `produced` for designs and evidence that establish the causal relation. Specify process evidence when using `enabled`, `constrained`, `shaped`, or `contributed to`.

## Frequency and significance

Report counts only when the sampling and coding process makes them meaningful. Do not infer importance from frequency alone. Mark whether an excerpt or case is:

- widespread across the corpus;
- recurring within a subgroup or setting;
- an analytically important exceptional case;
- a vivid illustration of a broader pattern;
- a counterexample that changes the claim.

Avoid `participants believed` when only a few did. State the subgroup, range, or condition that the data support.

## Quotations

- Copy quotations from primary material and check them against the source.
- Preserve wording and meaning; use brackets and ellipses transparently.
- Supply enough context to interpret the excerpt without exposing identity.
- Introduce the analytic purpose before or after the quote.
- Avoid strings of self-explanatory quotes. State the cross-case analysis in the author's voice.
- Track translation decisions and identify who translated the material.

## Negative cases and rival explanations

Search for cases that:

- reverse the proposed relation;
- show the same outcome through a different process;
- reveal a subgroup or stage where the theme does not hold;
- expose the researcher's initial assumption;
- support a plausible rival interpretation.

Use these cases to refine the conditions or mechanism. Document why they do or do not alter the central claim.

## Scope and transfer

Describe transfer through relevant features of the setting, actor relations, technology, institution, and time. Avoid claims about statistical representativeness unless the design supports them. State limitations where they affect validity, interpretation, or responsible use.

## Integrity check

Before delivery, verify that every quotation, count, participant descriptor, procedure, and ethics statement is present in the supplied source material. Mark all unresolved items for researcher verification.
