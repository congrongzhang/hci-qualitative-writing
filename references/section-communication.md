# Research Communication by Section

Use this guide to make each manuscript component answer the target reader's questions with a clear purpose, supported message, and proportionate detail. Pair it with the relevant literature, evidence, theory, or argument guide.

## Communication goal

Effective research writing helps a defined reader attend to, understand, evaluate, remember, and use a small number of supportable messages under constraints. Establish the purpose, reader, desired response, main message, and evaluation criteria before revising form.

Use a question-answer or claim-evidence design. Description supplies the `what`. Analysis explains the `so what` and the `why`. Keep enough background for the reader to understand the problem and remove details that do not advance the local question.

## Title and headings

A title should inform the target reader about the central issue and give a reason to continue. Choose among:

- a supported central finding;
- the research problem and response;
- a focused question when the answer depends on conditions.

Headings should state the section's purpose, question, or established answer. Topic labels such as `Background`, `Challenges`, or `Results 1` conceal the reasoning structure. Keep a topic label when venue conventions require it and add an informative subsection heading beneath it.

## Abstract

Answer these questions in a compact research story:

1. What broad need or phenomenon motivates the work?
2. What specific deficiency in knowledge or practice does the study address?
3. How did the study respond through its RQ, material, method, and analysis?
4. What central result did the analysis establish?
5. What knowledge gain and practical or theoretical significance follow?

Lead with the reader-relevant problem. Give enough method for readers to judge the result. Keep implications tied to the established outcome.

## Introduction

Move from the motivating need to the specific explanatory or practical deficiency, then to the research response and its value. Background earns space by supporting this rationale. End with the RQ, empirical response, central answer when appropriate, contribution, scope, and a roadmap that reflects the paper's actual argument.

Ask why the gap is worth resolving. Link the answer to a concrete HCI conversation, affected group, design decision, organisational practice, or governance problem.

## Related Work

Organise the review around focus questions and explanatory comparisons. Present the strongest current explanation before diagnosing its repeated failure. Use [literature-dialogue.md](literature-dialogue.md) for source-level and corpus-level work.

## Methods

Explain how the study pursued its objectives and why the choices let readers assess the resulting knowledge. For each consequential choice, connect the feature to its reason:

`choice -> rationale -> implementation -> effect on the evidence -> limitation or check`

Cover the empirical setting, recruitment or material selection, data generation, analysis, positionality, ethics, and material constraints at the level needed to evaluate the claims. Standard procedures need concise treatment. Unusual or load-bearing decisions need fuller justification.

## Findings

State what the analysis established and how the evidence produced that interpretation. Use analytic headings and arrange subsections through relations, processes, conditions, tensions, or boundary cases. A figure, table, or quotation supplies evidence. The prose explains the pattern and its relation to the RQ.

## Discussion

Begin from the central Findings and develop the explanation. State the relationship to prior work, conceptual consequence, HCI significance, rival interpretation, boundary, and justified next step. Keep methodological limitations next to the conclusions they affect or in a synthesis that names each consequence.

## Tables and figures

Give every display one clear purpose. The caption or nearby prose should state the question or message, define what readers see, identify the important pattern, and explain its relevance. Refer to the display at the point where the reader needs it. Remove visual and textual detail that does not support interpretation.

## Paragraphs and transitions

Use point, evidence, explanation, and link as functional checks. Connect adjacent sentences through shared terms and explicit relations. Connect paragraphs through a sequence of reader questions. A transition should identify the next reasoning need. Generic sequencing words cannot repair a missing relation.

## Iterative review

Review in separate passes:

1. research story and section questions;
2. argument and warrants;
3. evidence and claim scope;
4. cohesion across headings, paragraphs, tables, and figures;
5. sentence clarity and voice;
6. venue, citation, and submission checks.

Use a reverse outline after the first full draft. A break between passes can help the author see missing assumptions and confusing order.
