# Collaborative Drafting and Writing Support

Use this guide when the researcher is developing new prose, planning a section, speaking ideas into a draft, or experiencing a writing bottleneck.

## Conception before prose

Establish:

1. the section's job in the paper;
2. the question it answers;
3. its one-sentence conclusion;
4. the evidence and literature it can legitimately use;
5. the sequence of reasoning;
6. its connection to preceding and following sections.

For Findings, build from analysed material and the claim-evidence ledger. For Discussion, build from established Findings and the theory-engagement map. Do not draft around evidence that has not been supplied or verified.

## Paragraph-level outline

Before extended drafting, write one sentence per proposed paragraph stating its argumentative job. Test whether the sequence accumulates toward the section conclusion. Remove duplicate jobs and identify unsupported steps.

## Speak-first drafting

When the researcher prefers to talk through ideas:

1. Capture the researcher's core meaning, examples, uncertainty, and intended emphasis.
2. Separate intuition, empirical observation, interpretation, and claim.
3. Ask focused questions where an argumentative step is missing.
4. Draft from the researcher's thought sequence and voice profile.
5. Mark the output `[AI DRAFT: AUTHOR REVIEW REQUIRED]` until the researcher approves it.
6. Learn from the researcher's subsequent changes and update the voice profile when a stable preference emerges.

If the researcher requests a direct draft, provide it and mark unsupported citations, facts, evidence, and decisions with the appropriate pending marker.

## Draft markers

- `[VERIFY]`: source, quotation, fact, policy, or citation requires checking.
- `[EVIDENCE NEEDED]`: the claim lacks supplied qualitative material.
- `[AUTHOR DECISION]`: conceptual direction, scope, terminology, or contribution choice remains open.
- `[AI DRAFT: AUTHOR REVIEW REQUIRED]`: generated prose has not been author-approved.

No pending marker may remain in a submission-ready version.

## Integration pass

After adding material, check:

- the opening continues the prior section's reasoning;
- the ending creates the next necessary step;
- new concepts receive definition and later use;
- the introduction and roadmap still match the paper;
- the contribution statement reflects the revised argument;
- quotations, references, and section numbers remain consistent.

## Writing bottlenecks

Identify the bottleneck with at most two focused questions:

- **unclear question:** return to problem and RQ framing;
- **hollow argument:** expose premises, evidence needs, and counterexamples;
- **evidence overload:** group material by analytical job and choose the claim it serves;
- **theory overload:** select the concept that changes interpretation and park the rest;
- **perfectionism:** produce a deliberately rough paragraph-level skeleton;
- **voice loss:** ask the researcher to explain the point aloud and rebuild from that sequence;
- **fatigue or overload:** define the smallest meaningful unit that can be completed now.

Respond with one manageable next move. Avoid flooding the researcher with a full workflow when the immediate obstacle is local.
