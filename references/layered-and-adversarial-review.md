# Layered Critique and Adversarial Review

Use layered critique for diagnosis. Add adversarial review when the researcher wants a reviewer-style stress test.

## Four diagnostic layers

### 1. Research foundation

Check whether the paper stands as qualitative HCI research:

- importance of the phenomenon and precision of the problem;
- RQ, unit of analysis, sampling, data, and analytic fit;
- distinction among participant account, observation, interpretation, and theory;
- conceptual coherence and explanatory value of theory;
- ethics, positionality, reflexivity, and power relations;
- originality, significance, and traceability of the HCI contribution.

Foundation problems control the review. Pause sentence-level editing when the underlying claim, design, evidence, or contribution requires a decision.

### 2. Paper architecture

Check:

- cumulative progression from problem to contribution;
- section function and ordering;
- relation among Findings themes;
- balance of empirical material and interpretation;
- transition from Findings to Discussion;
- delivery of promises made in the abstract and introduction;
- consistency among title, abstract, contribution statement, and conclusion.

### 3. Paragraph reasoning

Check:

- the paragraph's job in the section;
- claim, evidence, interpretation, and link;
- conceptual drift or unmarked movement between evidence states;
- quotation context and analytic follow-through;
- relation to preceding and following paragraphs;
- necessity to the paper's argument.

### 4. Sentence and voice

Check:

- semantic precision and claim strength;
- natural syntax and cadence;
- terminology and translation consistency;
- citation integration;
- defensive framing, generic transitions, and formulaic AI prose;
- fit with the author's voice profile.

## Adversarial intensity

Use a 1–5 scale:

- **1, developmental:** surface the most constructive challenge and help formulate the question.
- **2, collegial:** test assumptions and missing links with moderate pressure.
- **3, peer review:** apply realistic CHI or CSCW scrutiny; default level.
- **4, selective venue:** pursue rival explanations, evidence gaps, and contribution weaknesses aggressively.
- **5, rejection case:** construct the strongest evidence-based case against acceptance.

State the level before the review. Increased intensity raises scrutiny and compression; it does not license invented defects or hostile language.

## HCI reviewer panel

Select the perspectives relevant to the task:

- **Contribution reviewer:** tests originality, significance, HCI relevance, and contribution naming.
- **Qualitative evidence reviewer:** tests design transparency, analytic grounding, negative cases, reflexivity, and scope.
- **Theory reviewer:** tests concept use, explanatory mechanism, levels of analysis, and claimed theoretical movement.
- **Sociotechnical and ethics reviewer:** tests actor relations, power, institutional conditions, harms, responsibility, and excluded perspectives.
- **Venue editor:** tests fit, completeness, policy compliance, and length-to-contribution ratio.
- **Informed outsider:** identifies tacit premises and passages a capable cross-domain reader cannot follow.

## Evidence contract for criticism

Every challenge must include:

- location or quoted claim;
- review perspective and diagnostic layer;
- specific concern;
- consequence for validity, interpretation, contribution, or readability;
- evidence from the manuscript or verified venue criteria;
- confidence: high, medium, or low;
- what would resolve or falsify the concern.

Mark speculative challenges as questions. Remove challenges that cannot be anchored.

## Reassess pushback

When the researcher disagrees, test the challenge against substantive information:

- clarified intended scope;
- additional empirical material;
- verified literature or theory;
- a plausible rival explanation;
- current venue criteria;
- revised wording that prevents the original reading.

Conclude with `stands`, `revised`, or `closed`, followed by the reason. A confident assertion alone does not settle the issue from either side.

## Review output

Report the overall assessment, foundation risks, structural risks, local issues, and author-decision questions. Batch three to five issues per round. Assign Fatal, Major, Moderate, or Minor severity and point to a specific repair path.
