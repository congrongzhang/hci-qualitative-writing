# Literature as Scholarly Dialogue

Use this guide to build Related Work and theoretical positioning from sources the researcher has supplied or explicitly asked to locate and verify.

## Map conversations, not authors

Group literature by the scholarly problem it addresses and the explanation it offers. For each conversation, record:

- central question and object of explanation;
- shared premises and key disagreements;
- empirical settings and methods supporting the claims;
- central concepts and their levels of analysis;
- what the conversation explains well;
- where its explanatory reach becomes uncertain for this study;
- the role it plays in the present paper.

Useful roles include empirical baseline, conceptual tool, methodological warrant, dialogue partner, competing explanation, boundary case, and bridge between literatures.

## Construct the positioning

Develop the Related Work argument through this sequence:

1. Establish the conversation the paper enters.
2. Explain the strongest available explanation of the phenomenon.
3. Identify the unresolved relation, process, condition, actor, or level of analysis.
4. Show why resolving it matters to HCI understanding or practice.
5. State how the study's data and analysis can address that problem.

A useful gap changes an explanation, decision, or research direction. An unstudied setting gains significance when it exposes a consequential assumption or mechanism.

## Avoid literature-list prose

Every paragraph should perform a comparative or argumentative job. Use citations to establish patterns, tensions, lineages, and limits. Avoid sequences where each sentence summarises a different paper without synthesis.

Distinguish:

- what a source explicitly claims;
- the researcher's synthesis across sources;
- the present paper's interpretation of a limitation;
- a proposal that still requires verification.

## HCI cross-tradition bridge

When combining HCI, CSCW, organisational studies, STS, information systems, or humanities theory:

- preserve each tradition's unit of analysis and concept meaning;
- identify where concepts are compatible, complementary, or in tension;
- explain the cost of moving a concept into a new empirical or disciplinary setting;
- specify which tradition supplies description, mechanism, norm, or design relevance;
- keep the HCI contribution visible after the bridge is built.

## Verification boundary

Use original texts for load-bearing claims and quotations. Mark remembered, secondary, or unverified descriptions with `[VERIFY]`. Do not attribute a position to an author from memory.
