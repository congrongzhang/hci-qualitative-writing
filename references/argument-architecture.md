# Argument Architecture

Use this guide before full drafting and whenever a manuscript contains several competing stories.

## Paper spine

Write these items in order:

1. One-sentence problem statement.
2. One research question or a tightly connected RQ set.
3. One-sentence answer supported by the study.
4. Two to four Findings claims whose relationship produces that answer.
5. The interpretive problem the Discussion resolves.
6. The primary contribution and its audience.
7. The strongest boundary condition or rival explanation.

If two items point toward different papers, identify the split and recommend a primary story.

## Section-function map

Assign one main job to each section:

- **Abstract:** problem, approach, central result, contribution, significance.
- **Introduction:** establish importance, locate the explanatory gap, state the study's answer and contribution.
- **Related Work:** construct the conversation the contribution enters; end with the unresolved analytical problem.
- **Methods:** make the knowledge-production process assessable, including sampling, setting, data generation, analysis, positionality, ethics, and material constraints.
- **Findings:** establish the patterns, relations, processes, mechanisms, and tensions that answer the RQ.
- **Discussion:** explain what the Findings change in prior knowledge, theory, design, or governance.
- **Conclusion:** consolidate the knowledge gain and its bounded significance.

## Findings sequence

Choose a relationship that carries explanatory value:

- temporal stages;
- actor or role relations;
- escalation or feedback loop;
- conditions and consequences;
- competing logics or tensions;
- levels of analysis;
- typical pattern followed by variation and boundary cases.

A sequence based only on interview-guide topics usually produces descriptive reporting. Reorganise around the answer to the RQ.

## Paragraph architecture

Give each paragraph one analytical job:

1. claim or orienting proposition;
2. evidence or literature needed for that proposition;
3. interpretation of the evidence;
4. link to the section's argument.

Quotes provide evidence and texture. The surrounding prose must state what the quote demonstrates and how it advances the analysis.

## Claim-evidence-contribution tracing

For every major claim, record:

- exact wording and claim level;
- source cases or materials;
- analytic operation connecting material to claim;
- variation, counterevidence, and alternative explanations;
- contribution supported;
- confidence and required revision.

Use the [claim-evidence-contribution ledger](../assets/claim-evidence-contribution-ledger.csv). Reduce claim strength, add analysis, or remove the claim when the chain remains incomplete.

## Coherence tests

- Every Findings heading should answer part of the RQ.
- The Discussion should rely on established Findings.
- Every contribution should have an identifiable Findings base.
- Every implication should cite the mechanism, relation, tension, or condition that motivates it.
- The abstract and conclusion should describe the same primary advance.
- The paper should remain intelligible after removing secondary concepts.
