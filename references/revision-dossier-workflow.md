# Revision-Dossier Workflow

Use this workflow for supervisor feedback, internal reviews, rebuttal preparation, revise-and-resubmit decisions, and examination corrections.

## One comment, one dossier

Preserve each source comment verbatim and assign it a stable ID. Split compound comments into linked dossiers when they require different evidence, manuscript locations, or decisions.

Each dossier records:

- source and exact comment;
- underlying concern;
- manuscript location;
- triage decision and rationale;
- evidence or analysis needed;
- planned change or defence;
- dependencies on other dossiers;
- revised text or response draft;
- verification criterion;
- status and owner.

Use [revision-dossier.md](../assets/revision-dossier.md) for complex comments and [review-response-matrix.csv](../assets/review-response-matrix.csv) as the master index.

## Triage

Classify the response:

- **Accept:** the concern is valid and requires a manuscript change.
- **Partially accept:** a focused change addresses the valid portion while preserving a supported choice.
- **Defend:** the current choice is supported; prepare an evidence-based explanation.
- **Apparent misreading:** re-read the manuscript to determine whether ambiguity enabled the interpretation. Add clarification when the text permits the reading.

Do not label a comment as misreading until the manuscript has been tested from the reviewer's perspective.

## Priority and dependency

Set priority by downstream impact:

- **P0:** changes the central claim, RQ, data interpretation, contribution, ethics, or venue eligibility;
- **P1:** materially improves method transparency, theory, Findings, Discussion, or reader comprehension;
- **P2:** local prose, presentation, or formatting.

Execute foundation and architecture dossiers before local edits. Group linked comments by section or analytical problem so terminology, evidence, and cross-references remain coherent.

When a revision changes the paper's story, update the communication brief, focus-question hierarchy, section headings, abstract, introduction, contribution statement, and conclusion as one dependency group.

## Definition of complete

A dossier closes when:

- the manuscript change or evidence-based defence is complete;
- the exact location is recorded;
- related sections and claims have been checked for consistency;
- citations, quotations, and evidence introduced by the revision are verified;
- response language accurately describes what changed;
- the master index and dossier status agree;
- a re-read confirms that the underlying concern is addressed.
- a reverse outline confirms that the revised passage answers its intended reader question and preserves the link to adjacent sections.

Generated revision prose alone does not close a dossier.

## Response register

Keep responses respectful, specific, and concise. Quote or identify the comment, state the decision, describe the change and location, and explain the evidence or reasoning. Thank reviewers when their observation materially improved the paper. Avoid repeated gratitude formulas and unsupported declarations that the issue has been resolved.

## Re-review

After major revisions, rerun the relevant reviewer perspective on the changed passages and their dependencies. Record `resolved`, `partially resolved`, or `needs rework` with a short rationale.
